## Key findings
<!-- Stage 03 (2026-11-07) from outputs/*.csv. NPS, % likely 988 and outcome_03 % agree hand-checked in Excel. -->
1. **Before → after change (retrospective items, n = 1,299):** participants reported higher
   knowledge after the training on all three items. The biggest gains were on 988 itself:
   - "I can describe 988 and how it works": 46% → 90% agree; 66% rated themselves higher
     (mean +1.11 on a 1–5 scale)
   - "I know when to use 988": 56% → 92% agree; 58% higher (mean +1.01)
   - "I can identify mental health challenges and crises": 72% → 90% agree; 44% higher (mean +0.46)
   - Overall, 77% improved on average (mean +0.86). Each change: Wilcoxon p < 0.001.
2. **Ceiling effect:** 14–22% of participants were already at the top score "before", so they
   could not improve. Among everyone else, the 988 items rose by +1.34 and +1.32 points
   (n = 1,117 and 1,018).
3. **Likelihood to call 988 (n = 293):** 73% scored 7–10 on a 0–10 scale (mean 7.66). The
   question was only asked from April 2026 on.
4. **Recommend (n = 1,266):** mean 7.59 of 10, Net Promoter Score +12. Students, who are 83%
   of respondents, are far less enthusiastic (NPS −2, n = 1,051) than other roles:
   community members +80 (n = 94), caregivers/parents +78 (n = 69), school staff +84 (n = 67).
   Students still reported gains similar to other groups (mean +0.81 vs +0.83 to +1.32).
5. **Differences by group (descriptive; groups with n ≥ 10 only):**
   - **County:** Prince George's NPS +56 (n = 291) vs Anne Arundel −5 (n = 923). Mean change
     was +1.04 vs +0.77.
   - Differences in change across counties and across roles are unlikely to be chance
     (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.001 for both). Across languages they are not significant
     (p = 0.052; Spanish n = 19).
   - County differences may partly reflect how many students each county trained; this
     wasn't tested.
6. **Training quality (% agree):** presenters engaged participants 88% (n = 1,125), knew the
   subject 90% (n = 1,123), training well organized 92% (n = 1,118).
7. **Over time:** monthly groups from Oct 2025 to Mar 2026 (n = 28–42 each) recommended the
   training at 9.3–9.5. Larger groups from Apr 2026 on scored 6.9–8.2 (n = 27–363). This
   may reflect a shift toward student audiences; we haven't verified that.

## Methods & limitations
- n = ___ responses, ___ to ___ (training dates)
- Retrospective pre-test: self-report, recall bias, no control group
- Groups with n < 10 suppressed; missing data handled per item
- About 2% of answers to the three training-aspect items used two non-standard labels
  ("Very agree", "Satisfied"), probably from an older form version. They were treated as
  missing, because where "Satisfied" falls on an agree/disagree scale is unclear
- The "likely to call 988" question was added to the form later: it was first answered on
  April 8, 2026, and only 293 of 1,299 respondents (23%) answered it. Results for that
  question describe this later subset only. "Likely to call 988" means a score of 7–10 on
  its 0–10 scale
- The 293 people who answered the 988 question recommended the training slightly more than
  the other 1,006 (mean 7.88 vs 7.50, Mann-Whitney p = 0.016), but reported the same
  before → after change (+0.88 vs +0.85, p = 0.84)
- Role groups overlap (14 people chose more than one role), so role results don't sum to
  the total, and the role significance test is approximate
- Group comparisons are descriptive. Differences between counties or roles may be driven
  by other factors (such as the share of students) and are not causal
- County spelling variants (e.g. "X" vs "X County") were merged before grouping
- "None" / "N/A" answers to the open-text questions were kept as answers ("nothing to
  change"), not treated as missing
- Theme coding is keyword-based and validated on a hand-coded sample of ___
